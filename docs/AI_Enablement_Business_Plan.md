## One-page summary

#### The problem

Companies have AI, but their people don't use it well. Most workers have had no employer training. Staff use personal accounts with company data. Paid seats go unused. In Vietnam, 74% of firms that use AI only use it at a basic level, and 54% say the main barrier is skills.

#### What we do

We **assess** where AI fits, **train** each team hands-on with its own tasks, **build** the custom assistants and integrations they need, and **measure** usage and hours saved before and after.

#### Why us

Most AI trainers can teach prompting but can't build. Engineering firms can build but can't teach a room of finance staff. We do both: a business-trained founder who leads sales and client work, and two engineers who build agents and MCP servers, all working in English and Vietnamese.

#### How we start with no capital

Low-priced entry offers, the first paid client within 30 days, founding-client discounts in exchange for case studies, and free startup credits for tools. Founders take no pay until monthly revenue crosses an agreed level.

#### Where we start

**Vietnam** (language advantage, the new AI and data protection laws, low delivery cost) and **Australia** remotely (same time zones as SEA, high prices). Then Malaysia, the Philippines, Indonesia and the US.

#### Targets ASSUMPTION

First paid client by Dec 2026. 5 case studies by Mar 2027. About $230K revenue in year 1, $750K in year 2 and $1.8M in year 3. Founders paid from about month 5–6.

Green light

Demand is well documented in every target market. Recent laws in Vietnam, Malaysia and Australia give buyers a deadline. Your team's ability to both teach and build is a real advantage, and sales has a clear owner. The biggest risks are now: **all selling rests on one founder**, **workshop delivery competes with selling for the same founder's time**, and **there is no starting capital to live on** while the business gets going. The founder decisions below address all three.

## Decisions for the founding team

Discuss these together this week. Each has a recommendation, but they are your calls.

#### 1. Who delivers workshops with three founders?

Sales is covered: the business/PM founder owns cold calls, cold email and closing. Building is covered by the two engineers. No founder's main job is **delivery**: running workshops for non-technical staff and leading change in client teams.

Recommendation: the sales founder leads client-facing sessions, since running meetings and managing stakeholders as a PM carries over well. One engineer co-trains and runs the hands-on build parts. Keep at least 60% of the sales founder's week for selling. Once revenue passes about $15K a month, bring in a bilingual contract trainer paid per session, and make it a full-time hire in Q2–Q3 2027.

#### 2. Roles and titles

Recommendation: one clear owner for each area, even if everyone delivers at first.

| CEO / Growth | Sales pipeline, cold outreach, partnerships, pricing, the company's public voice | \[Your name\]: business degree, PM, IT/CS background |

#### 3. Equity and founder agreement

**Decided:** three founders, with equity split equally (one third each).

Still recommended: put it in a simple founder agreement before the first client, with **4-year vesting and a 1-year cliff**, all IP assigned to the company, and clear rules for a founder who leaves. Consider setting aside a small option pool (e.g. 10%) for future key hires such as a lead trainer. Have a lawyer review the agreement in the country where you incorporate.

#### 4. Where to register the company

This depends on where the founders live and who you'll invoice first.

- Vietnamese clients usually need a local VAT e-invoice, which means a Vietnamese entity or a local partner who invoices for you.
- Australian and US clients can be served from abroad. Australian GST registration is only needed above A$75K in Australian sales.
- Malaysian government-subsidized training (HRD Corp) requires a Malaysian company (Sdn Bhd).

Recommendation: register one company, in the country where most founders live. Use partners to invoice in other countries until revenue justifies a local entity. Confirm with an accountant.

#### 5. Personal runway and the founder-pay trigger

With no starting capital, each founder needs to cover their own living costs at first.

Recommendation: each founder shares how many months they can last without pay. Agree a rule, for example: "founders draw $\[X\]/month each once monthly revenue exceeds $\[Y\] for two months running." Paid build projects are the fastest source of cash. Prioritize them if anyone's runway is under 4 months.

#### 6. Where to start

Recommendation: Vietnam first (foreign-owned and outsourcing firms, priced in USD), and Australia remotely at the same time. See [Markets & sequence](#markets). Don't actively sell in more than two markets until there are 5 case studies.

#### 7. Company name

Recommendation: pick a name that works in English and Vietnamese, has a .com or .ai domain available, and doesn't clash with existing AI-training brands in VN, AU, MY or the US. Shortlist 10, check domains and trademarks, decide by week 2.

## Problem–solution framework

### The problem, by market

| Signal | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Workers use AI without training | US: only about 1 in 3 got employer AI training; 36% say training is enough (BCG 2025). AU: 48% trained (Randstad 2026). VN: 81% use AI daily, but 54% of firms say skills are the main barrier (e-Conomy SEA 2025, AWS 2025). DATA |
| Usage is shallow | 74% of AI-using firms in Vietnam, 67% in Malaysia and 78% in the Philippines use AI only at a basic level (AWS 2025–26). DATA |
| Shadow AI | 78% of AI users bring their own tools (Microsoft WTI, global and AU). Shadow AI is involved in 43% of breaches (IBM 2026, via a secondary source). DATA |
| No plan from leadership | US: about 25% of employees have heard a clear AI plan (Gallup 2026). AU: 28% say leaders are aligned (Microsoft WTI 2026). MY: 24% of firms have formal AI governance (AWS). DATA |
| New legal pressure | VN: AI Law in force since Mar 2026; personal data protection law since Jan 2026, with fines up to 5% of revenue. AU: disclosure of automated decisions required from 10 Dec 2026. MY: amended data protection law requires a data protection officer. DATA |
| Root cause | General-purpose assistants only create value when someone maps them to specific tasks, connects them to company systems, sets rules and builds habits. In most mid-sized firms nobody owns that work. EST |

### The solution

A 90-day engagement:

- **Week 1:** survey and interviews show who uses what, where time goes, and what data is at risk.
- **Week 2:** leadership signs an AI policy aligned with local law and an approved tool list.
- **Weeks 3–6:** each team practices on its own real work, in English or Vietnamese.
- **Build:** we build their custom GPTs, Claude Projects and Gems, and where useful an MCP connector so the assistant can read their CRM or accounting data safely.
- **Ongoing:** internal champions keep it going.
- **Day 90:** a report shows usage and hours saved against the baseline.

### Insight

AI adoption is a behavior-change problem and an integration problem. Courses teach prompting, but usage sticks only when people practice on their own work, know the rules, and have AI connected to the systems where their work happens.

Trainers solve the first part, IT providers half-solve the last. Doing both in one engagement is what sets us apart. The BCG evidence supports this: 79% of staff with 5+ hours of practice become regular users, compared with 67% of those with less.

- **Vietnam first.** Being fluent in Vietnamese is an edge few competitors have, delivery costs are low, and two new laws create urgency. - **US last, as an active market.** It is the biggest and most crowded. Take inbound leads from content and the founders' networks, and push actively once case studies exist.

## Go-to-market

### First 10 clients (founder-led, no ad spend)

- **Warm network:** each founder lists 30 people who own, run or manage a 20+ person business, in VN, AU or the US. Ask for a 20-minute conversation, not a sale.
- **Free bilingual webinars, monthly:** "Your company's AI policy under Vietnam's new AI Law" (Vietnamese) and "Getting value from the Copilot seats you already bought" (English). Each one feeds the Scorecard.
- **Show what you build:** post short demo videos on LinkedIn and Facebook of real assistants (a Claude Project that writes a weekly report, an MCP server that lets ChatGPT read a CRM). This is your strongest proof.
- **Founding-client offers** as above.

### Channels after that

1. **Partners who own the client relationship:** IT providers, accounting and audit firms, HR consultancies, chambers of commerce (AmCham, EuroCham, KoCham and JCCI in Vietnam; local chambers in AU). Offer a 15–20% referral fee.
2. **Vendor partner programs:** Claude Partner Network, Microsoft and Google partner directories.
3. **HRD Corp-registered providers in Malaysia:** deliver as their subcontractor.
4. **Outbound to similar firms** once you have a case study in their industry.

### Sales motion

Scorecard or webinar → 30-minute call → Kickstart or Assessment (small, quick yes) → readout proposing the Sprint and a Build → retainer at day 90.
